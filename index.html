Internal Linking Audit

Industry
Chartered Surveyors

This was a piece of work I completed for a client at Mountex Digital. From an initial review, the internal linking structure across the site was inconsistent. Some pages didn't link to related services at all, missing opportunities to guide users towards commercial content. In other cases, links were present but used over-optimised anchor text, detracting from readability and user trust. Finally, most links appeared in the footer or navigation rather than in the body copy, meaning they carried less weight for SEO.

My recommendations

  • De-optimise some anchor text across a cluster of commercial pages. This made the language more natural for Google and for readers.
  • Add internal links to mentioned geographical locations across the site. This helped signal the company's locale and provided more ways for users to easily navigate around the site.
  • Many 'similar services' were mentioned on each commercial page, but not linked at all. I rewrote the copy to go from a formulaic bullet list, to descriptive body copy and was then able to recommend naturally placed anchor text and links to promote similar services across the site.
  • An orphaned page with commercial value was also identified during my audit. I browsed the site and suggested a cluster of genuinely relevant pages to link to it so Google and users could find and navigate to this valuable page.
  • The boiler-plate 'Why choose us' component did not link out to any further reading. I recommended adding a recurring link to the About Us page so users could remain engaged and informed about the company anywhere on the site.
  • Some pages had thin content, leaving very little scope for internal links. Where this was the case, I provided new copy and links to insert onto the pages.

On-page Audit

Industry
Financial services

This was a piece of work I completed for a payroll services client at Mountex Digital. This client's most important service page was only ranking for branded terms, meaning new customers would struggle to discover the brand. The page lacked a clear direction as there were two separate search intents being targeted. As a result, topical relevance was diluted and rankings were primarily branded rather than non-branded.

My recommendations

The core solution I proposed was to have two separate commercial pages, dedicated to each search intent, with unique content and keyword targeting.

  • Highlighted which keywords are receiving the most searches to outline why the changes need to take place to capture new customers.
  • Outlined the plan to have two separate commercial pages, one dedicated to 'self-employed payroll services' and the other to 'CIS Payroll services'.
  • Recommended page titles and H1s for the two new service pages. These were written to target high-volume keywords to capture new audiences that might not have discovered the brand yet.
  • For both pages, I crafted fresh copy to sell each of the services, include valuable internal links and mention essential keywords. This was in bullet point form as this strategy was working for top-ranking competitors for the target keywords. Bullet lists, when used thoughtfully can be more digestible for users looking for quick information.

Competitor Analysis

Industry
IT Services

This was a piece of work I completed for an IT & Cybersecurity client at Mountex Digital. Key services for this client included the cybersecurity and IT consulting pages. These were poorly designed and struggling to stand out in a crowded market. A competitor analysis was the best way to identify any gaps or new opportunities to optimise the commercial content.

My recommendations

Due to the nature of this particular business, hyper-local analysis was necessary to figure out the true competitors and tactics winning search. The core solution I proposed was to have heavily localised city/town specific pages for their core service offering.

  • Showcased that competitors were winning search with localised service pages, so I recommended replicating what was already working. The new location pages must be highly customised to avoid repetition or duplicate content.
  • The broad service pages were outdated. Across both the cybersecurity and IT consultancy niches, I noticed competitors were employing these tactics to signal stronger EEAT signals, offer a better UX or improve the site hierarchy/topical authority:
    • Free Consultation sticky banner on all service pages
    • Breadcrumbs in place across all services pages
    • Targeting of high-volume keywords within page title, H1 and body copy
    • Links to Meet the Team and About Us pages
    • Clear signposting to industry-standard certifications and valuable business partnerships
    • Use of customer testimonials to strengthen trust signals
    • The addition of a cybersecurity 'disaster recovery' service page
